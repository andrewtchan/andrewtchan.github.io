I've always been a little bit fascinated by the concept of "tea" -- as in the distinct light meal of small foods and hot tea usually taken in the late afternoon. Like many of my most long-lasting fixations I think it probably has its roots in those staples of English children's fantasy literature: mainly <cite>The Hobbit</cite>, but also The <cite>Harry Potter</cite> series^[I have since renounced my ties to this franchise due to the author's bigoted views.], etc. I loved those scenes where in the midst of a long and arduous journey, the protagonists are allowed the brief respite of a warm meal by the fire, to break into a precious reserve from home, buried deep in the pocket of a tattered coat, lovingly wrapped up and carried along through miles of rain and snow, through dense forests and over impossibly tall mountains, miraculously surviving fierce battles and mad dashes to safety. While their troubles may be far from over, for just a moment they can rest as the challenges of the day are allowed to drift ever so slightly out of mind.

These sorts of scenes came back to me frequently throughout my undergrad. Those nights where I'd be so innundated with work that I'd have to force myself to step away for just 15 minutes to have something to eat that day. Even when my body and mind were completely wracked with stress, I found true comfort and shelter in those brief pauses. Those ever-present deadlines had no choice but to politely wait for me to finish. After all, what kind of messed up world would have Bilbo ambushed while he was enjoying his second breakfast?? I bookmarked this {% include "helpers/external-link.html" text:"essay about the magic of food in fiction" href:"https://womenwriteaboutcomics.com/2017/01/food-in-film-finding-safe-spaces-in-fiction/" %} a long time ago which does a much better job than I can of articulating this idea.

So to bring us back to the point of this post: I love the idea of taking tea (the beverage) and turning it into the much more indulgent, fanciful Tea (the meal). A break during the day to stop and replenish yourself, and perhaps be a little bit extra. Well I'm sure the English take it quite seriously, and I do love that for them.

Final note before the reviews. While searching for the header image for this post I found this {% include "helpers/external-link.html" text:"lovely little blog by Nicola Thomis" href:"https://www.fragrantica.com/news/Scents-of-England-Tea-and-Biscuits-19857.html" %} about English tea culture, biscuits, and an unexpectedly captivating review of several tea- and biscuit-inspired perfumes. You should totally check it out :D It's obviously a pretty light-hearted subject but you can tell the author is extremely knowledgeable about fragrances which makes it very compelling.

## McVitie's Digestives: <b>7/10</b>

When I hear "tea biscuit", this is what I think of. Very much a British classic. The flavor is quite well-balanced between salty and mild sweetness. I generally love sweet things but I need them to be "not too sweet"^[Shout out to the Asians out there.]. Especially for a biscuit, I'm looking for the kind of sweetness that coffee people refer to -- more like the pleasant absence of harshness than the presence of any sort of strong sucrose-like flavor. IYKYK. Definitely <em>not</em> a cookie. It just feels like a lot to be eating cookies at 3pm on a regular basis.

{% include "helpers/figure.html" name:"mcvities.jpg" alt:"A single McVitie's Digestive biscuit on a small wooden cutting board." caption:"A lush McVitie's Digestive biscuit" %}

With that said, I feel many of you may enjoy this biscuit even less than I do. As the name suggests they are quite fibrous and wheat-y in the mouth. I personally don't mind that texture but I can see how it could be off-putting. Though I don't think they actually have all that much fiber, nutritionally speaking. Another matter is that these biscuits are so, so crumbly. You really have to eat them over the sink or garbage bin which kind of takes away from the "afternoon tea" experience. I certainly wouldn't bring one of these to my desk.

Finally, for any dunkers out there (I wouldn't typically describe myself as such), the McVitie's Digestive is not the one. It disintegrates upon contact with hot liquid and leaves a bunch of bloated crummy bits in the bottom of your cup. Unless you're into that sort of thing, in which case you should absolutely check this one out.

<u>Overall rating:</u> solid staple depending on your mood/appetite, but not my first choice.

## Britannia Bourbon: <b>4/10</b>

Another titan of English teatime. The Bourbon is new to me -- I heard about it recently in Max Miller's ({% include "helpers/external-link.html" text:"@TastingHistory" href:"https://www.youtube.com/@TastingHistory" %})^[My mom and I love his YouTube channel and I own his {% include "helpers/external-link.html" text:"historical recipe cookbook" href:"https://www.barnesandnoble.com/w/tasting-history-max-miller/1141673843?ean=9781982186180" %}.] {% include "helpers/external-link.html" text:"review of UK Biscuits/Cookies in collaboration with Sorted Food" href:"https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cD41X8ebNrk" %}, which partly inspired this post.

{% include "helpers/figure.html" name:"bourbon.jpg" alt:"A single Britannia Bourbon biscuit on a small wooden cutting board." caption:"A middling Britannia Bourbon biscuit" %}

The appearance of the Bourbon, with its dense texture, dark hue, and little sprinkles of salt, as well as Max's impressions in the video ("It's really mild... they forgot the sugar!"), really led me to believe that this biscuit was made for me as a huge fan of dark chocolate, things with subtle sweetness, and of course, biscuits. So I was hugely disappointed when I took my first bite and found it to be overwhelmingly sweet and dominated by the flavor of the chocolate cream between the two biscuit pieces. One of the hosts in the video even went so far as to describe this thing as having a "slight chocolate bitterness" to it. I was shocked. It honestly tasted like an Oreo to me. I love Oreos for what they are but clearly I was expecting something else here.

I'm aware I probably have an unusually sugar-sensitive palate, but I also started to wonder if I had unknowingly purchased some kind of an "Americanized" version. I'll continue that thought when I get to the Jaffa cake.

<u>Overall rating:</u> nice biscuit texture, aesthetically pleasing, immense potential, but ultimately very disappointing.

## Bakers Tennis Classic Coconut Biscuit: <b>9/10</b>

I don't actually know whether these are as "classic" as the previous two (and as the name would suggest), and I didn't bother to research it. I found them at World Market in a clearance bin while hunting for the Bourbons and Jaffa cakes. All I know is that this biscuit freaking rocks. The coconut comes through wonderfully and as you might expect, they're not-too-sweet. These were nice and crunchy, light, a little buttery. Notice how I am referring to them in the plural, which is because I downed about half a dozen right after trying the first one.

My one complaint is that I had to battle a bit to get these out of the sleeve. The packaging is really tight and you kind of have to chip away at it with scissors. Not sure if it's because they were shipped overseas or something like that. But be careful because I definitely broke a few pieces trying to free them from the wrapper.

{% include "helpers/figure.html" name:"tennis.jpg" alt:"A single Bakers Tennis biscuit on a small wooden cutting board." caption:"A banging Bakers Tennis biscuit" %}

<u>Overall rating:</u> top notch flavor and texture, also were very cheap -- I'll be buying more soon.
